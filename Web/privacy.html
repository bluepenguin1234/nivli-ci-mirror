Privacy Policy

Effective date: September 2026

Applies to Nivli for iPhone, version 3.0 and later. Earlier versions of Nivli were a different product; the previous policy no longer applies.

The short version: Nivli does not collect any data. The apps you choose, your workouts, your streak and your settings stay on your iPhone. There is no account, no server of ours, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party code. Nivli never learns the names of the apps you locked, and anything it reads from Apple Health stays on the device. The App Store privacy label for Nivli is Data Not Collected.

What Nivli stores, and where

Everything Nivli keeps is written into a storage area that belongs to the app on your iPhone (an App Group, so the app and its two Screen Time extensions can read the same file). None of it is sent to us or to anyone else. We have no way to see it.

  • The apps that wait. When you pick apps, categories or websites, iOS hands Nivli a set of opaque tokens. A token is a meaningless identifier that only iOS can resolve. Nivli stores the tokens so it can ask iOS to shield the same things tomorrow. It cannot read an app's name, icon, bundle ID or your usage from a token, and neither can we. When the app shows you the list, iOS draws the names and icons; Nivli only supplies the tokens.
  • Your workout log. For each workout: the date, the start time, the length in minutes, the kind (run, walk, strength and so on) and whether you logged it by hand or it came from Apple Health.
  • Your streak. Worked out on the device from the workout log and your rest days. The current and longest streak are kept so the app can show them.
  • Your settings. Your minimum workout length, your weekly rest days and any day you took off, whether Apple Health and reminders are on, the reminder time, your appearance choice, the answers you gave during onboarding, and whether the subscription is currently valid.

Nivli's data is stored in a file that is excluded from iCloud Backup and computer backups, because it can contain workouts read from Apple Health. Setting up a new iPhone means setting Nivli up again; your streak does not move between phones.

Screen Time (Family Controls)

Locking apps uses Apple's Screen Time API. Here is exactly what that means.

  • The authorization. On first run Nivli asks iOS for Screen Time authorization for this iPhone's own user (Apple calls this individual authorization). You approve it in a system prompt. Nivli has no parental controls and no family or child features, and it never requests authorization for another person's device.
  • What Nivli can do. Two things: show you Apple's own app picker, and ask iOS to put a shield over the apps, categories and sites you picked in that picker. A background extension re-applies the shield at midnight so a new day starts locked.
  • What Nivli cannot see. It cannot see which apps you chose, how long you use any app, what you do inside an app, your notifications, your browsing, or any Screen Time report. The selection arrives as opaque tokens and stays that way.
  • What is reported. Nothing. Nivli sends no information about your selection or your app use anywhere, because it has nowhere to send it.
  • Removing the shields. Clear your selection in Nivli's Settings, turn Screen Time authorization off, or delete the app, and iOS lifts the shields. Nivli also lifts them by itself when your subscription is not valid, when onboarding was never finished, when the selection is empty, on a rest day, and once you have worked out. It never locks anything you did not pick.

One honest limit: a shield stops an app from opening. It cannot close an app you already have open. iOS shows the Nivli lock screen the next time you open it.

Apple Health

Connecting Apple Health is optional. Nivli works perfectly well with workouts you type in yourself.

  • Read-only, workouts only. Nivli asks for permission to read workouts. It asks for nothing else, and it never writes anything to Health.
  • What it uses. For a workout on the current day: its start time, its length, its type, and its identifier so the same workout is not counted twice. That is the whole list.
  • Where it goes. Nowhere. The workout is read on your iPhone, turned into an entry in Nivli's own on-device log, and used to decide whether today is unlocked. It is never transmitted, and we never receive it. Apple's rules for HealthKit data say it must not be used for advertising, marketing or sale to anyone; Nivli does none of those, has no advertising, and has no one to sell to.
  • Background delivery. With your permission, Health can wake Nivli when a new workout is recorded, so a watch run unlocks your apps without you opening the app. The wake-up carries the same workout data and is handled entirely on the device.
  • Turning it off. Open the Health app, then Sharing › Apps › Nivli, and switch Workouts off. You can also use iPhone Settings › Privacy & Security › Health › Nivli, or turn Apple Health off inside Nivli's Settings. Nivli then stops importing. Workouts it already imported stay in Nivli's log until you reset it or delete the app.

Notifications

Notifications are optional and are asked for once, during onboarding. There are two kinds, both scheduled locally on your iPhone: a note that your apps have unlocked when a workout arrives from Health, and an evening reminder at the time you choose if you have not moved yet. They contain nothing else. Nivli has no push server and receives no remote notifications. Decline, or turn them off later in iPhone Settings › Notifications › Nivli, and the rest of the app works the same.

Purchases

Nivli is sold as one auto-renewable subscription through Apple's in-app purchase system. Apple handles the payment, your Apple Account, receipts, renewals and refunds, under Apple's own privacy policy. Nivli learns one thing from Apple: whether the subscription is currently valid on this device. It never sees your payment card, billing address or Apple Account details, and it keeps no purchase records anywhere except that valid-until date on your phone. Manage or cancel in iPhone Settings › [your name] › Subscriptions.

Children

Nivli is a general-purpose app for adults managing their own screen time on their own iPhone. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone of any age, because we do not collect personal information at all. Nivli is not a parental-control app: it has no family or child features, it cannot be installed on or used to restrict someone else's device, and its Screen Time authorization covers only the iPhone it is running on.

Your choices

  • Unlock everything. Clear your selection under Settings › Apps that wait and nothing is shielded any more.
  • Take a day off. Set weekly rest days, or use Take today off, under Settings.
  • Stop Apple Health. Turn it off in Nivli's Settings, or in Health › Sharing › Apps › Nivli.
  • Stop notifications. Turn reminders off in Nivli's Settings, or in iPhone Settings › Notifications › Nivli.
  • Delete your data. Settings › Reset › Turn off Nivli and delete my data erases your workouts, streak, selection and settings from this iPhone.
  • Delete the app. Removing Nivli removes its storage with it, and iOS lifts the shields.

Since we never receive your data, there is no data we could hand over, correct or erase on request. The only copy is on your iPhone.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Nivli ever changes how data is handled, for example by adding a service run by us, this page and the App Store privacy label will be updated before that version is released, and the effective date above will change.
